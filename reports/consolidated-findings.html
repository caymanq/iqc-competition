Report 1 · Initial 2033 quantum checkpoint

Paper Phase Transition — Tipping-Point Map (Vasion / IQC 2026)

A 20-industry Ising model solved with QAOA (Classiq, 2-layer, 20 qubits) and classical simulated annealing. The quantum optimizer converged to an expectation value of -2.64 (approx. ratio ~0.52 vs. SA ground state -5.11 for year 2033). The tipping-point map shows three distinct waves: an early digital vanguard (2029), a mid-decade retail/hospitality cluster (2032), and a massive 2036 cascade where 11 industries flip simultaneously, pulled over by strong partner ties.

The Model

Each of 20 U.S. industries is a node with spin s_i in {-1 (paper), +1 (digital)}. The economy each year settles into the minimum-energy Ising configuration:

Energy = -sum J_ij s_i s_j - sum (A(year) - r_i) * s_i

where J_ij = 0.15 x tie-strength (24 partner ties), r_i = industry resistance (0.10 to 0.90), and A(year) = 0.07 x (year - 2026) is the AI pressure ramp. The first year a node comes out digital is its flip year.

Key finding: both methods agree on which industries are digital in 2033 (the 5 lowest-resistance nodes). The QAOA expectation value is shallower than the SA ground state because p=2 layers cannot fully resolve the energy landscape for 20 qubits with 24 couplings — a known NISQ limitation. The Story — Who Tips, Who Pulls Whom

Wave 1 — The Digital Vanguard (2029): Technology, Media, and Telecom flip first. Their resistances (0.10-0.25) are so low that AI pressure alone (0.21 by 2029) pushes them over. They are lightly coupled to the rest of the network, so they flip without waiting for partners.

Wave 2 — The Consumer Cluster (2032): Retail and Hospitality flip together. Retail's tie to Media (J=0.045) and Hospitality's tie to Retail (J=0.06) provide just enough pull to bring them over two years before AI pressure alone would.

Wave 3 — The Supply Chain (2034): Logistics, Manufacturing, and Energy flip as a unit. Logistics is pulled by its strong ties to Retail (J=0.12) and Manufacturing (J=0.135). Energy follows Manufacturing (J=0.075). This is a classic supply-chain cascade.

Wave 4 — The Outlier (2035): Agriculture flips one year early, pulled by its Logistics tie (J=0.09) before AI pressure alone would move it.

Wave 5 — The Great Cascade (2036): Eleven industries flip in a single year. By 2036, AI pressure (0.70) exceeds the resistance of every remaining industry. But the timing is not coincidental — the Healthcare-Insurance tie (J=0.15, the strongest in the network) means both flip together. Banking-Real Estate (J=0.12), Legal-Government (J=0.09), and the entire professional-services cluster all cross the threshold simultaneously. A business that lands Healthcare in 2035 gets Insurance almost for free.

Where Quantum Helped — and Where It Did Not

Helped: The Ising model maps directly onto a quantum cost Hamiltonian with no reformulation overhead. The QAOA circuit naturally encodes all 24 partner ties as ZZ interactions and all 20 field terms as single-qubit Z rotations. The optimizer converged in 46 iterations and correctly identified the 2033 ground state (same 5 digital industries as SA). For a quantum annealer (D-Wave), the embedding would be even more natural — the problem is a native QUBO.

Did not help (honestly): For 20 nodes, a laptop solves this exactly in under a second. The QAOA approximation ratio of ~52% at p=2 is well below what SA achieves. The gap closes as p increases, but circuit depth grows with it — a real NISQ device would add noise that degrades the ratio further. The quantum advantage, if any, emerges at scale: hundreds of industries across dozens of regions, where the Hilbert space is too large for classical exact methods and SA may get stuck in local minima.

Paper Phase Transition — Full 2026–2040 Quantum Sweep

A 2-layer QAOA circuit on 20 qubits was optimized independently for each of the 15 years 2026–2040 using a single synthesized Classiq program, with year-specific Pauli Hamiltonians and warm-started parameters. The quantum sweep reproduces the classical tipping-point map exactly: five distinct waves culminating in a massive 11-industry cascade in 2036. The QAOA approximation ratio improves from ~12% in 2026 to a peak of ~77% in 2035 as the problem landscape sharpens, then declines again as all industries go digital and the energy well deepens.

The Full Quantum Sweep — QAOA Expectation Value 2026–2040

One synthesized 20-qubit QAOA circuit (program ID: 3LlKSNEbSJtq0ePjXHJozG6OjAU) was reused for all 15 years. Each year's Ising Hamiltonian was encoded as 44 Pauli terms (20 Z-field terms shifting by +0.07/year, plus 24 fixed ZZ coupling terms). Parameters were warm-started from the previous year's optimum, reducing iterations needed to converge.

The Story — Five Waves, One Big Year

Wave 1 (2029) — The Digital Vanguard: Technology, Media, and Telecom flip first. Their resistances (0.10–0.25) are so low that AI pressure alone (A=0.21) pushes them over. They are lightly coupled to the rest of the network, so they flip without waiting for partners. A sales team should be in these industries by 2027 at the latest.

Wave 2 (2032) — The Consumer Cluster: Retail and Hospitality flip together, two years before AI pressure alone would move them. The Media-Retail tie (J=0.045) and Hospitality-Retail tie (J=0.06) provide just enough pull. Target these in 2030–2031.

Wave 3 (2034) — The Supply Chain: Logistics, Manufacturing, and Energy flip as a unit. Logistics is pulled by its strong ties to both Retail (J=0.12) and Manufacturing (J=0.135). Energy follows Manufacturing. Landing one lands all three.

Wave 4 (2035) — The Outlier: Agriculture flips one year early, dragged over by its Logistics tie (J=0.09) before AI pressure alone would move it. A surprise early mover.

Wave 5 (2036) — The Great Cascade: Eleven industries flip in a single year. The Healthcare-Insurance tie (J=0.15) — the strongest in the entire network — is the trigger. Once Healthcare crosses, Insurance follows immediately. Banking-Real Estate (J=0.12), Legal-Government (J=0.09), and the entire professional-services cluster all cross the threshold simultaneously. Government (r=0.85) and Legal (r=0.90) — the two most stubborn industries — are dragged over by their partners despite never wanting to flip on their own. 2036 is the year to have already closed these deals.

The New Story — Geographic Contagion

The key new story from multi-region scaling is regional contagion. California's lower resistance (-0.12 shift) means its cascade cluster flips in 2034 instead of 2035. That one-year head start propagates outward via inter-region ties:

California flips 2034 → Pacific NW follows 2034 (CA-PNW tie, J=0.05 per industry) Pacific NW flips 2034 → Mountain West follows 2035 (PNW-MW tie) California flips 2034 → Southwest follows 2035 (CA-SW tie via Southwest-California adjacency) Texas follows 2035 (TX-SW tie)

This is a geographic cascade — the same wave structure as the industry cascade, but now spreading across space. A sales team that lands Healthcare in California in 2033 gets not just Insurance and Pharma in California, but also Healthcare in Pacific NW one year later for free.

Agriculture in California is the most dramatic example: it flips in 2033 — two full years earlier than in any other region — because California Logistics is already digital by 2033 and the Agriculture-Logistics tie (J=0.09) is strong enough to pull it over immediately.

Quantum Scaling Picture

At 100 qubits, the QAOA approach changes character significantly:

Gate-based (Classiq simulator): A 100-qubit, p=2 QAOA circuit is synthesizable but the Hamiltonian now has 220 ZZ terms + 100 Z terms = 320 Pauli terms. The circuit depth grows proportionally, and the approximation ratio at p=2 will be lower than the ~60% we saw at 20 qubits. Running 15 years × 40 optimizer iterations = 600 circuit evaluations — feasible on a simulator but slow.

Quantum annealer (D-Wave): This is where the 100-node problem becomes a natural fit. The QUBO maps directly onto the annealer's native Ising problem. The embedding (finding which physical qubits represent which logical qubits) needs to be done once and reused across all 15 years — exactly as the challenge spec suggests. D-Wave's Advantage system has ~5000 qubits, so 100 logical qubits is well within range.

Scaling further: At 500+ nodes (25 industries × 20 regions), classical SA starts to slow down and may miss global optima. That is the regime where quantum hardware begins to offer a genuine computational argument, not just a structural one.
